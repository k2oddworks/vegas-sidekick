Oprah is taking over Sphere. But this isn’t a concert.

Oprah Winfrey’s AHA is a two-hour immersive live experience built around Sphere itself — with music, film, sensory effects and four performances in April 2027.

Sphere has already proven it can reinvent the concert. Oprah Winfrey wants to see what happens when you take the concert out of the equation entirely.

Oprah Winfrey’s AHA arrives at Sphere April 2–4, 2027 for four performances. It’s being described as an immersive live experience combining music, film, sensory effects and live performers — with Winfrey using the room’s technology to build something around awe, connection and the kind of personal realization that gave the project its name.

The idea traces back to U2’s opening night at Sphere in 2023. Winfrey was there, saw what the building could do, and started thinking about whether the same technology could be used for something built around personal reflection rather than a traditional concert.

AHA is currently scheduled for April 2, 3 and 4. Winfrey has said more dates are possible if the concept works.

What actually happens during AHA?

The announced creative team gives you a better idea of what this is than trying to squeeze it into a normal Vegas-show category. Grammy winner Jacob Collier, poet and author Ocean Vuong, musician Ajeet and gospel singer Wintley Phipps are among the announced performers.

The wider creative group includes composer Max Richter, musicians Jason White and Jon Hopkins, playwright Tarell Alvin McCraney and stage designer Es Devlin. The production is expected to use Sphere’s enormous screen, spatial audio and sensory capabilities as part of the experience rather than simply as a backdrop.

“What makes this interesting to me isn’t just that Oprah Winfrey is coming to Las Vegas. It’s that she looked at Sphere and saw a completely different use for it. We’ve watched that building become a concert venue, a movie experience and one of the biggest attractions in Vegas. AHA pushes it into another category again. That’s good for Las Vegas, because Sphere is at its best when somebody walks in and asks, ‘What else can we do with this thing?’”
— Kris Kidd, Founder, Vegas Sidekick

It’s also another test of what Sphere can become

That may be the most interesting Vegas part of this announcement. Sphere’s identity is still being written. Residencies have shown what it can do for artists. Its film experiences have shown it can operate as an attraction. AHA is another experiment: can the room support a live format that isn’t really a concert, keynote or conventional stage show?

Las Vegas keeps finding new uses for the room

Sphere is becoming a format, not just a venue.

The Vegas Sidekick take

AHA doesn’t need to become a permanent Sphere residency to matter. The interesting part is that another major creator looked at a building originally associated with giant concerts and saw a different medium entirely.

If that keeps happening, Sphere becomes more useful to Las Vegas than a venue with a spectacular screen. It becomes its own category of entertainment — one where the question isn’t simply who is performing there next, but what somebody is going to invent for it next.
