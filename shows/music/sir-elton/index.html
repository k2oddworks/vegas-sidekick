About Sir Elton

Sequins, glasses, and a piano — the Elton John catalogue up close.

Sir Elton is an Elton John tribute built around the instrument that made him: the piano. "Rocket Man," "Tiny Dancer," "Bennie and the Jets," "Your Song" — five decades of hits at the Athena Showlounge inside Alexis Park Resort.

🌵
Spike's Take Monday and Tuesday only — and that's precisely why it's worth knowing about. Those are the two nights when half the Strip goes dark and everyone's stuck choosing between the same three shows. This is $40, it's ten minutes off the Strip, and the catalogue is bulletproof. Put it on the shortlist for the start of a trip rather than the end.

The costumes and the glasses are all present and correct, but the show lives or dies on the playing, and that's where it puts its weight. Monday and Tuesday nights only, which makes it a useful option on the two evenings when the Strip's calendar thins out.

What to Expect

🎹 Built Around the Piano
Elton's catalogue is piano-first, and so is this. The show is arranged around the playing rather than treating it as backing for a costume parade.
🕶️ The Full Wardrobe
The glasses, the sequins, the feathers. Elton's stage look is half the story and the show knows it — expect changes through the night.
📻 Five Decades of Hits
"Your Song" through "I'm Still Standing" and out the other side. It's a catalogue almost nobody dislikes, which makes it an easy pick for a mixed group.
📅 A Monday–Tuesday Option
Two nights a week, at the exact point in the week when Strip options get thin. Worth remembering when you're trying to fill an early-trip evening.

🪑 Sweet Spot
The tables in the center of the showroom — the Sweet Spot. You're close to the stage, you've got room for a drink, and you're perfectly placed to get up and dance, which this room makes easy. For a show in a room this size, a table beats a row of seats every time. Book one and bring a group.
The two Athena reserved blocks directly in front of the stage — the closest seats to the cast. Great if you want to be right up on the performance and don't mind trading the table for proximity. A solid pick for superfans.
Booths 1 through 4 along the sides of the room — a more private, tucked-in option for a small group that wants its own space. You'll view the stage at a slight angle, but you get a dedicated booth and an easy spot to keep your party together.
General Admission at the back of the room — first-come, first-served seating and the value option. Arrive a little early for a better spot. Perfectly good for a budget-friendly night; if you want to be in the thick of it, upgrade to a table.
