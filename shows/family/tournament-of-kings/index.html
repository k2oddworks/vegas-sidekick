Dinner, horses and a very loud arena

Tournament of Kings puts the audience around a central arena floor, divides the room into kingdoms and gives each section a knight to cheer for. The performance mixes horse work, jousting, sword combat and fire effects while dinner is served directly to your seat.

The meal is part of the ticket rather than an add-on: Cornish hen, potatoes, corn, a biscuit and apple-square dessert, plus included nonalcoholic drinks under the current menu. You eat with your hands by design. It sounds stranger before the show than it feels once everyone around you is doing it.

🌵 Kris’s take

This is one of the easiest family-show recommendations because the activity never really stops and dinner solves another part of the night. The tradeoff: it is loud, rowdy and intentionally messy. Anyone hoping for a quiet meal followed by a polished theater production is shopping for the wrong thing.

What to expect

Live jousting

Armored performers charge across the arena on trained horses, followed by staged sword combat and other physical sequences.

Dinner at your seat

The medieval-style meal is served during the production and is included with the ticket.

Your section has a side

You are assigned a kingdom and encouraged to cheer loudly for its knight. The crowd participation is part of the atmosphere.

Fire and elevated sound

Pyrotechnics and a loud arena mix are part of the show. Worth knowing for very young kids or anyone sensitive to noise.

What is actually for dinner?

The current meal includes slow-roasted Cornish hen, herb-roasted potatoes, corn on the cob, a biscuit and an apple-square dessert. Nonalcoholic beverages are included; cocktails and premium drinks are available separately. The standard experience is utensil-free, though accessibility accommodations can be requested.

Where should you sit?

Romania / Dragon

These center sections provide the most direct head-on relationship to the jousting runs and are a good default when the view matters more than squeezing the price.

Spain / Austria

A side-angle view of the arena. You still see the action clearly, but the horse charges read from a different perspective.

Back rows

Do not panic about being “far back.” The arena has only a handful of rows, so even the rear remains physically close to the performance floor.
