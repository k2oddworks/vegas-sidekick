The 30-second answer

Is KÀ worth seeing?

Yes if you want Cirque at its most cinematic — a giant moving stage, martial-arts choreography and a real adventure story instead of a loose collection of acts.

KÀ is one of the Cirque shows where the theater matters almost as much as the performers. The central platform rises, tilts and turns into a near-vertical wall while the cast fights, climbs and falls across it. The production is more narrative than most Cirque shows, following twins separated during an attack and moving through a fantasy adventure.

🌵 Kris’s take

If someone says “I want Cirque” and wants something dramatic, KÀ is an easy place to start. The downside is that it is more serious and story-driven than the lighter Cirque shows. If you want pure act-after-act acrobatics with less plot, Mystère is the cleaner fit.

Is it for you?

Pick KÀ if this sounds like your night

Good fit

  • You want a big, dramatic Cirque production.
  • You like action, aerial work and martial-arts-style choreography.
  • You want the stage engineering to be part of the spectacle.
  • A clear adventure story sounds better than disconnected acts.

Think twice if…

  • You want the lightest, funniest Cirque show.
  • You prefer pure act-after-act acrobatics with almost no plot.
  • You want an intimate cabaret room rather than a giant custom theater.

Before you go

KÀ FAQ

Tickets currently start at $80. Your date and seat determine the final total.
KÀ runs about 90 minutes with no intermission.
The regular schedule is Monday through Wednesday at 7:00 PM and 9:30 PM, plus Saturday and Sunday at 4:30 PM and 8:30 PM. Thursday and Friday are dark. Confirm your exact date at checkout.
Guests must be at least 3 years old. Guests under 18 should attend with an adult. The show is highly visual, though some younger children may find the battle sequences intense.
KÀ Theatre is inside MGM Grand at 3799 S Las Vegas Blvd. MGM Grand is large, so allow extra time to reach the theater.
Center seats are the safest all-around choice because the giant moving platform and full-stage compositions are easier to read head-on. Closer seats add intensity; farther back gives you more of the whole machine at once.
