Zombie movie logic, Vegas revue energy

Zombie Burlesque imagines a version of 1950s Las Vegas where the undead won and somehow opened a nightclub. The theme gives the show a reason to mix burlesque numbers, physical comedy, specialty acts and live music without pretending any of it needs to make normal sense.

The horror element is mostly costume and camp. This is not built around jump scares or gore. The production wants laughs, energy and a slightly weird late-night Vegas feeling.

🌵 Kris’s take

This is a strong value pick if you like adult comedy but do not want a straight revue. The tradeoff is that the theme is deliberately cheesy. If “1950s zombie cabaret” sounds dumb rather than fun to you, the show is not going to talk you out of that.

What to expect

Comedy first

The zombie apocalypse is a framing device for jokes and variety acts rather than an attempt to scare the audience.

Burlesque + specialty acts

The show moves between dance, comedy and physical variety instead of staying in one revue format.

A live band

The music is played live, which gives the production more immediate energy than a fully tracked show.

Compact runtime

Seventy-five minutes with no intermission. The format works well if this is one stop in a longer Vegas night.

Where should you sit?

VIP Quarantine Zone

The closer section is roughly the front portion of the room. It is worth considering here because facial comedy, choreography and practical effects read better at shorter distance.

General Admission

A reasonable budget option in a compact theater. You can still follow the show; the compromise is losing some of the small physical details.

Finding V Theater

V Theater is inside Miracle Mile Shops at Planet Hollywood, not directly off the main casino floor. On a first visit, arrive at Planet Hollywood about 45 minutes early so you have time to navigate the mall and get settled.
