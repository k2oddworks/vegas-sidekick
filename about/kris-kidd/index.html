Background

Why I built Vegas Sidekick

For years, my job was helping visitors decide what to see in Las Vegas. That gives you a different view of the city than reading a press release or scraping star ratings.

I’ve worked in Las Vegas show ticketing since 2006 and later spent years in high-end resort concierge work. I’ve seen well over a hundred shows on and off the Strip, watched productions open and close, and had thousands of conversations with visitors trying to figure out what actually fits their night.

Vegas Sidekick exists because the useful answer is rarely just “this show has good reviews.” The useful answer is whether it fits you: your budget, your group, your sense of humor, your kids, your dinner plans, and how much patience you have for a three-hour spectacle.

I’m not trying to make every show sound amazing. I’d rather tell you who should book it, who should skip it, what seat I’d buy, and the downside you should know before paying.

Editorial standards

How my recommendations work

Say the downside

If there’s a real reason a show may not fit you, it belongs on the page.

Separate price from value

Cheap doesn’t automatically mean good, and expensive doesn’t automatically mean a rip-off.

No fake urgency

No invented scarcity, countdown tricks or pretending every ticket is about to disappear.

Use firsthand Vegas context

Venue, neighborhood, runtime, audience and what else is nearby can matter as much as the production.

Correct things when they change

Prices, schedules, casts and openings change. We update the useful fact without making routine corrections into a story.

Disclose the business model

Vegas Sidekick may earn a commission on eligible ticket bookings. That never buys a positive recommendation.

About the site

Independent recommendations, with a business model you can see

Vegas Sidekick is an independent Las Vegas entertainment guide. Some show links are affiliate links, which means Vegas Sidekick may earn a commission when a booking is completed through them at no extra cost to the customer.

That relationship is disclosed because it should be. It also doesn’t buy a positive review. If I think a show is wrong for a certain audience, I’ll say it. If I think a cheaper seat is the smarter buy, I’ll say that too.
