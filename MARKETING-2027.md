# Vegas Sidekick — 2027 Marketing Plan

Prepared November 27, 2026. The creative identity is approved; cadence, targets, channel priorities and tests below are recommended operating defaults, not commitments to spend or guaranteed outcomes. Governed by [BRAND.md](BRAND.md), [SPIKE-CHARACTER-BIBLE.md](SPIKE-CHARACTER-BIBLE.md), [SOCIAL-TEMPLATES.md](SOCIAL-TEMPLATES.md), and [GOOD-TO-KNOW.md](GOOD-TO-KNOW.md).

## 1. Objective and positioning

Make Vegas Sidekick recognizable before a traveler needs tickets, then earn the booking handoff through genuinely useful advice. The brand is the local friend who works in tickets, not a ticket seller or generic tourism listing.

Creative direction: **The Insider With a Weird Side.** Bold modern surrealism, selective retro Vegas, credible information, deadpan humor.
- Vegas Sidekick: **Vegas has a show for that.**
- Spike: **You'll never want to do Vegas without Spike.**
- Creative test: make people stop because it's weird, remember because it's Spike, and visit because it's useful.

Primary business outcome: attributable affiliate bookings and commission revenue when partner reporting is available. Qualified visits and verified outbound ticket clicks are intermediate outcomes, not bookings. Reach, followers and likes are diagnostic, not the business outcome.

## 2. Audiences and messages

| Audience | Need | Content and destination |
|---|---|---|
| First-time Vegas visitors | Reduce choice overload | Situation-based recommendation; relevant guide or show page |
| Repeat visitors | Find something different | New announcements, specific recommendations; relevant show or calendar page |
| Value-conscious show shoppers | Choose the right ticket, not merely the cheapest | Verified deal or seating guidance; show or deals page |
| Vegas locals and entertainment professionals | Reliable changes and announcements | Good to Know and entertainment calendars; appropriate information page |

Do not assume demographics or fabricate audience sizes. Use actual engagement and conversion evidence to refine these hypotheses.

## 3. Content mix

Starting allocation by number of published social posts, measured over a rolling month; time-sensitive verified updates may override it.

| Pillar | Share | Examples |
|---|---:|---|
| Useful entertainment information | 40% | Good to Know, Just Announced, calendar highlights |
| Creative entertainment and humor | 30% | Spike's absurd situations, fictional commercials |
| Show recommendations and discovery | 20% | Spike's Pick, Vegas has a show for that |
| Direct promotional content | 10% | Verified ticket value, branded booking invitation |

The split is a planning hypothesis from the creative discussion, not a required weekly quota. Within the creative-ad subset, test unexpected locations (30%), fictional commercials (25%), ordinary life (20%), entertainment insider (25%). Do not confuse this separate ad mix with the total social mix.

## 4. Channel strategy

- Facebook: primary launch channel for shareable information, recommendations and short creative videos. Use the refreshed profile and cover; publish useful destination links where relevant.
- Instagram: reuse the strongest concepts in portrait graphics and vertical short videos, adapted to the platform rather than mechanically reposted. Use clear profile destination and relevant captions.
- Other existing social channels: selectively adapt proven work only when it serves the audience and complies with community rules. No link dumping or undisclosed promotion.
- Website: destination for specific show choices, useful calendar information and verified ticket handoffs. Do not send every visitor to the homepage when a more relevant page exists.

These are initial channel priorities, subject to actual performance. No new accounts, paid spend, automation or publishing authority is implied.

## 5. Sustainable operating rhythm

Start with three planned posts per week across the primary channel, plus genuinely material announcements when needed. A video may replace a planned post, rather than increasing the workload.

| Slot | Suggested purpose |
|---|---|
| Early week | Practical update or announcement |
| Midweek | Show recommendation or situation-based pick |
| Weekend | Spike creative/humor or timely useful information |

Use a rolling monthly mix rather than forcing a category when nothing worthwhile exists. Start with two original creative videos per month and reuse their stills or crops where appropriate. Cap planned production at a proposed four hours per week; simplify output when work exceeds the cap. Batch concepts and templates, not factual verification. Never invent updates to fill a slot.

## 6. Campaign roadmap

| Period | Focus | Deliverable and decision |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Recognition and baseline | Establish consistent assets, test three recurring series plus Spike videos, baseline engagement and qualified traffic |
| Q2 | Repeatable formats | Expand the strongest series, test recommendation-led landing destinations and creative hooks |
| Q3 | Seasonal relevance | Adapt proven formats to actual summer/fall entertainment and visitor needs; retire weak formats |
| Q4 | Consolidation | Use verified holiday and new-year planning information, review annual results, lock 2028 priorities |

Seasonal angles are hypotheses, not advance claims about specific shows, prices or schedules.

## 8. Creative production

1. Start with one objective, one audience and one message. Select a template or a custom Spike concept.
2. Verify any factual claims and use approved show photos or artwork. Use the canonical Spike avatar as a visual reference for fictional creative.
3. Make the joke visually clear without explanation; use real brand assets and a relevant destination. The end card is optional for still posts and useful for video.
4. Review readability, crop, logo placement, image rights, factual accuracy, disclosure and mobile presentation. No fake scarcity, testimonials or endorsements.
5. Publish manually, log results and retain the final asset. Do not auto-publish or fabricate facts.

Affiliate relationships must be clearly disclosed when a post recommends or promotes affiliate-linked products; place disclosure where readers will notice it, not buried among hashtags. Do not imply Vegas Sidekick sells tickets or controls inventory.

## 9. Measurement and attribution

Use available platform insights, first-party site analytics and affiliate reporting. Do not assume a particular analytics event already exists; verify instrumentation first.

For website campaign links use UTM parameters on the Vegas Sidekick destination, e.g. utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=2027_q1&utm_content=spike_coffee_v1. Preserve canonical site paths and existing affiliate referral URLs exactly; do not append campaign parameters to partner links without confirmed support. Never place personal information in campaign identifiers.

| Metric | Role |
|---|---|
| Reach and video watch/completion | Attention |
| Shares and saves per reach | Useful or memorable content |
| Link clicks and attributed website sessions | Interest; reconcile reporting differences |
| Engaged visits and outbound ticket clicks | Qualified discovery and purchase intent |
| Reported affiliate bookings and commissions | Business outcome, where available |
| Production time per asset | Sustainability |

Baseline the first 30 days before setting numerical growth targets. Use comparable formats and observation windows (suggested seven days for social post comparison, monthly for business review). Keep an optional simple log: date, channel, series, creative ID, destination, UTM, production minutes, reach, shares, saves, visits, outbound clicks, partner outcomes if available. Missing attribution is unknown, not zero.

## 10. Review and decision rules

Weekly: check factual corrections, creative performance and production time. Monthly: compare content families, review qualified traffic and affiliate outcomes, then pick one variable to test next month. Quarterly: retain, revise or retire formats.

Compare at least four reasonably comparable executions before treating a creative signal as meaningful; small samples are directional. Do not abandon useful customer-service posts just because they generate fewer clicks. A funny video that earns attention but never connects to the brand needs a clearer brand cue or destination, not simply more views.

Paid advertising is not part of the initial rollout. Consider it only after organic creative and attribution are validated, with a separately approved budget and measurable objective. No spend authorized here.
